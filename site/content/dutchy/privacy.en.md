# Privacy Statement

*Last updated: 11 November 2026*

## The short version

Dutchy has no account and no adverts, and does not track you across other apps or websites. Your progress, notes and word lists live on your own device. What the app sends: usage statistics without your name (below; you can turn them off in Settings), the purchase check under Payments, and your phone’s speech recognition when that works online.

Two things deserve an honest explanation, and they are below: speech recognition does not always happen on your device, and the app sends usage statistics to PostHog.

## Speech recognition

When you do a speaking exercise, Dutchy uses the speech recognition built into your browser or operating system. Dutchy only gets the text back; we receive no audio and store nothing.

However: in a browser, Chrome and Safari send that audio to Google and Apple respectively to turn it into text. That happens under their privacy terms, not ours.

If you want certainty that no audio leaves your device, skip the speaking exercises — the rest of the course works regardless.

Dutchy itself records nothing and keeps no audio, not even briefly.

## Text to speech

Dutchy reads Dutch aloud with recordings of a natural voice. The recordings for A1 and A2 are inside the app. Those for the other levels are downloaded only if you choose to, from our website (nightowly.dev, which is hosted by GitHub). During such a download GitHub sees your IP address, as with any visit to a website; nothing about you or your progress is sent along. Where there is no recording, Dutchy uses your device's own voices. Some platforms also offer cloud voices; you choose which voice to use in Settings, where the local ones are marked.

## AI feedback

In Examen you can have a writing or speaking answer checked by AI. That happens only when you ask for it, and the first time Dutchy asks for your consent first. The task and your text (for speaking: what your phone heard) then go through our server at Cloudflare to the AI service that does the checking. The app names that service and its country before you agree, and asks again if the service changes. At present it is DeepSeek, in China: a country outside the EU where your data is less protected than here, which is why we ask for your explicit consent. What DeepSeek does with the text falls under their terms. Your name, your progress and your install number are not sent. Our server does not keep your text; it only counts how many checks there are per day, per network address as a code that lasts one day. Do not put real personal details in a text you have checked.

## What is stored on your device

In your browser's or app's storage, Dutchy keeps: your progress and review schedule, your settings, your word bank and notes, the texts you have written, your per-sound pronunciation profile, and your name if you entered one.

That stays there. There is no sync, no backup held by us, and no way for us to reach it. Only what Usage statistics lists leaves the device, and that is never the contents of this storage.

You can export all of it to a file and erase all of it, both from Settings. Erasing is immediate and final.

## Usage statistics

To see where the course works and where people get stuck, the app sends short notes of what happens to PostHog (PostHog, Inc., United States), a product analytics service. We use PostHog’s EU environment, which stores the data in the EU (Frankfurt). Such a note says: the app opened; you finished the introduction, a lesson, a review or a round, with how many were right first time and how much XP; you saw the subscription screen or subscribed; the app asked the store for a rating; or you gave a thumbs up or down after a lesson.

Each note carries the app version, the kind of device (iPhone, Android or web), your level, whether you subscribe, and a random number made for this install, so we can see how far people get. If you entered a creator’s code (or came through a link with one), it is there too, so we know through whom people find Dutchy. Never your name, your email address, your answers, your notes, your word lists or your texts. After a thumbs down you may type a short comment yourself; it is sent with it, so please leave personal details out.

Like any server you connect to, PostHog sees your IP address. The app asks PostHog not to derive a location from it.

Legal basis (GDPR): our legitimate interest in improving the course. You can turn this off at any time in Settings → Je gegevens en back-up → Help Dutchy beter maken. The app then sends no more statistics, and anything still waiting is dropped.

PostHog handles this data only to provide its service to us, under its data processing terms, and keeps it for as long as we use its service. PostHog’s own policy: https://posthog.com/privacy

## Payments

The subscription runs through Apple or Google. They process the payment; we never see your payment details.

To know whether you hold a valid subscription, the app uses RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc., United States), a subscription-management service. It receives: a random identifier made for this install of the app (never your name, email address or phone number); on iPhone plus Apple’s “identifier for vendor”, an ID iOS gives one developer’s apps on your phone; your purchase and subscription status and the purchase receipts from Apple or Google; a creator’s code, if you entered one; the technical data needed for this: app version, app ID, phone model, operating system version, language and country or storefront; and technical reports about the check itself: how long it took and which error codes the store returned. Like any server you connect to, RevenueCat sees your IP address whenever the app connects.

This happens when the app starts or comes back on screen, when you open the subscription screen, and when you buy or restore. It is used to unlock, restore and keep track of your subscription, to catch fake receipts, and to show us purchase figures (purchases, renewals, cancellations). It is never used for adverts or tracking, and no decisions about you are made automatically. Your progress, notes, word lists, texts and pronunciation profile never go to RevenueCat.

Legal basis (GDPR): performing the agreement with you, which is the subscription you take, and our legitimate interest in catching fake receipts and keeping purchase figures.

RevenueCat handles this data only to provide its service to us, under its data processing terms. Apart from the speech recognition and the usage statistics described above, no company other than RevenueCat and Apple or Google gets data from the app. RevenueCat processes the data in the United States; transfers from the EU, the UK and other places with rules on sending data abroad rely on the safeguards set out in its data processing terms. RevenueCat keeps the data for as long as we use its service, or until you ask for it to be deleted, under its own retention terms. RevenueCat’s own policy: https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy/

A purchase belongs to your App Store or Google Play account. The “Aankopen herstellen” (Restore purchases) button on the subscription screen asks the store for your purchases and links them to this install. You do not sign in to Dutchy for this.

We do not sell or share personal information, as California law uses those words, and Dutchy does not track you across apps or websites.

In the web version nothing can be purchased, so none of this happens at all.

## Sending feedback

In Settings you can send a short report showing where you got to in the course: counts of units, lessons, reviews and mock exams. It contains no name, no email address, no notes and nothing you have written.

You see the whole thing before sending, and the app does not send this report on its own: you send it yourself through your own mail or share sheet. If you do not, nothing happens.

If something about a question seems wrong, you can report it at your answer with the little flag. That opens your own mail app with a message to us: the question, your answer, what you think is wrong and the app version. You send that yourself too.

## Cookies and tracking

Dutchy sets no cookies and contains no advertising or tracking code: nobody follows you through Dutchy across other apps or websites. The usage statistics above keep their random install number and their queue in the app’s storage, only for our own figures. The app loads no course content from the internet while you use it: the whole course is in the file you downloaded. The network traffic is the purchase check under Payments, the usage statistics when they are on, and your phone’s speech recognition when that works online.

There is no cookie banner: there are no cookies, and you turn the statistics off in Settings.

## Children

Dutchy is suitable for all ages and asks nobody for a name, email address or other details, children included. All that is sent is the purchase check under Payments and the usage statistics, which you turn off in Settings.

## Your rights

Under the GDPR you have rights of access, correction, erasure, objection, restriction and portability. We do not hold your progress, so you exercise most of those rights yourself: export gives you access, and erase in Settings is deletion.

The usage statistics hang on a random install number, not on your name, so we cannot look them up per person. You object with the Help Dutchy beter maken switch in Settings: after that nothing more is sent.

To have RevenueCat delete the purchase records about your install, email us which store you bought from and the order number from your purchase confirmation. If you have an active subscription, cancel it first and then uninstall Dutchy.

You can always complain to the Dutch Data Protection Authority (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl) or to the supervisory authority in your own country.
