# Privacy Statement

*Last updated: 1 November 2026*

## The short version

Dutchy has no account, no server of ours, no analytics, no adverts and no tracking. Your progress, notes and word lists live on your own device, and we cannot see them. Apart from speech recognition (below), the only thing the app itself sends is the purchase check described under Payments.

One thing deserves an honest explanation, and it is below: speech recognition does not always happen on your device.

## Speech recognition

When you do a speaking exercise, Dutchy uses the speech recognition built into your browser or operating system. Dutchy only gets the text back; we receive no audio and store nothing.

However: in a browser, Chrome and Safari send that audio to Google and Apple respectively to turn it into text. That happens under their privacy terms, not ours.

If you want certainty that no audio leaves your device, skip the speaking exercises — the rest of the course works regardless.

Dutchy itself records nothing and keeps no audio, not even briefly.

## Text to speech

Reading Dutch sentences aloud uses your device's own voices. Some platforms also offer cloud voices; you choose which voice to use in Settings, where the local ones are marked.

## What is stored on your device

In your browser's or app's storage, Dutchy keeps: your progress and review schedule, your settings, your word bank and notes, the texts you have written, your per-sound pronunciation profile, and your name if you entered one.

That stays there. There is no sync, no backup held by us, and no way for us to reach it.

You can export all of it to a file and erase all of it, both from Settings. Erasing is immediate and final.

## Payments

The subscription runs through Apple or Google. They process the payment; we never see your payment details.

To know whether you hold a valid subscription, the app uses RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc., United States), a subscription-management service. It receives: a random identifier made for this install of the app (never your name, email address or phone number); on iPhone plus Apple’s “identifier for vendor”, an ID iOS gives one developer’s apps on your phone; your purchase and subscription status and the purchase receipts from Apple or Google; the technical data needed for this: app version, app ID, phone model, operating system version, language and country or storefront; and technical reports about the check itself: how long it took and which error codes the store returned. Like any server you connect to, RevenueCat sees your IP address whenever the app connects.

This happens when the app starts or comes back on screen, when you open the subscription screen, and when you buy or restore. It is used to unlock, restore and keep track of your subscription, to catch fake receipts, and to show us purchase figures (purchases, renewals, cancellations). It is never used for adverts or tracking, and no decisions about you are made automatically. Your progress, notes, word lists, texts and pronunciation profile are never sent.

Legal basis (GDPR): performing the agreement with you, which is the subscription you take, and our legitimate interest in catching fake receipts and keeping purchase figures.

RevenueCat handles this data only to provide its service to us, under its data processing terms. Apart from the speech recognition described above, no company other than RevenueCat and Apple or Google gets data from the app. RevenueCat processes the data in the United States; transfers from the EU, the UK and other places with rules on sending data abroad rely on the safeguards set out in its data processing terms. RevenueCat keeps the data for as long as we use its service, or until you ask for it to be deleted, under its own retention terms. RevenueCat’s own policy: https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy/

A purchase belongs to your App Store or Google Play account. The “Aankopen herstellen” (Restore purchases) button on the subscription screen asks the store for your purchases and links them to this install. You do not sign in to Dutchy for this.

We do not sell or share personal information, as California law uses those words, and Dutchy does not track you across apps or websites.

In the web version nothing can be purchased, so none of this happens at all.

## Sending feedback

In Settings you can send a short report showing where you got to in the course: counts of units, lessons, reviews and mock exams. It contains no name, no email address, no notes and nothing you have written.

You see the whole thing before sending, and the app transmits nothing on its own: you send it yourself through your own mail or share sheet. If you do not, nothing happens.

## No cookies, no tracking

Dutchy sets no cookies, uses no analytics and contains no third-party trackers. The app loads no course content from the internet while you use it: the whole course is in the file you downloaded. The only network traffic is the purchase check under Payments, plus your phone’s speech recognition when that works online.

That is why there is no cookie banner. There is nothing to accept or refuse.

## Children

Dutchy is suitable for all ages and asks nobody for a name, email address or other details, children included. The only thing sent is the purchase check described under Payments.

## Your rights

Under the GDPR you have rights of access, correction, erasure, objection, restriction and portability. Because we hold nothing of yours ourselves, you can exercise most of those rights yourself: export gives you access, and erase in Settings is deletion.

To have RevenueCat delete the purchase records about your install, email us which store you bought from and the order number from your purchase confirmation. If you have an active subscription, cancel it first and then uninstall Dutchy.

You can always complain to the Dutch Data Protection Authority (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl) or to the supervisory authority in your own country.
